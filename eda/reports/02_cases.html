Takeaways

  • 2011–2012 were outbreak years. Iloilo had 1,088 and 854 cases (typical year: 100–250), and Cagayan de Oro had 667 in 2012, around Typhoon Sendong (Washi, Dec 2011).
  • Cases come in sharp spikes: Iloilo's worst week (Sep 2011) had 189 cases. Any model must cope with rare, very large weeks, and the robustness checks should test results with and without these years.
  • Zamboanga's jump to 168 in 2022 is another isolated outbreak.

3. Seasonality¶

In [5]:
# Outbreak years excluded so one storm doesn't define the "typical" year.
typical = d[~d.year.isin([2011, 2012])]
grp = np.where(typical.city == ILOILO, ILOILO, "Other 11 cities")
month = typical.groupby([grp, typical.date.dt.month]).cases.sum().unstack(0)
share = month / month.sum()
ax = share.plot.bar(color=[BLUE, ORANGE], width=0.8, figsize=(10, 3.8))
ax.set_xticklabels(pd.date_range("2000-01", periods=12, freq="MS").strftime("%b"), rotation=0)
ax.yaxis.set_major_formatter(plt.matplotlib.ticker.PercentFormatter(1, decimals=0))
ax.set(title="Share of a typical year's cases by month (2011–12 excluded)", xlabel="", ylabel="share of cases")
ax.grid(axis="x", visible=False)
ax.legend()
plt.show()

Takeaway: cases peak in the wet season, August–November. Iloilo peaks hard in October (~29% of a typical year's cases). The seasonal shape is shared across cities, so the model needs a seasonal term. Otherwise "it rained" and "it's September" get confused.

4. Distribution: many zeros, rare large counts¶

Takeaways

  • Most city-weeks are zero (28% in Iloilo, over 75% in most cities).
  • Variance is far above the mean (Iloilo: 33×), so the counts are overdispersed. A Poisson model would be overconfident. A negative binomial model fits this shape.
